## Discussion Summary

### Topic 1: What Went Well
- The project constitution was established for specification-driven development (SDD).
- The sprint was well structured, with clearly defined objectives and deadlines.
- Branch protection was added on main, so every merge required a review by another team member. 8 of 9 merged pull requests had an approving review, up from none in Sprint 1.
- The workload was more evenly distributed.
- Specs were written before code and reviewed by multiple team members.

### Topic 2: What Didn't Go Well
- Meetings were sparser this sprint. SDD made implementation faster but reduced collaboration, and communication within the team was almost nonexistent compared with Sprint 1. The team attributes this largely to the change in course structure after Sprint 1.
- Deadlines were missed.
- Pull requests waited two to five days for review and approval, which held up the work that depended on them. Team members also have other academic and personal obligations.
- The PostgreSQL migration still hasn't happened.
- SDD meant less hands-on understanding of the code.

- Rotating responsibilities for the final sprint is under discussion, though it may not be logistically feasible. The Sprint 3 kickoff meeting will center on this decision.
